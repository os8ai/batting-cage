# Why — Working Document

## Per-element research and options

### Problem statement
**Research (precedent: moderate):** No incumbent occupies this exact niche (premium desktop-browser one-button cage game), but four adjacent framings cover every ingredient the product combines. (1) Cage-tech (HitTrax, Toptracer) frames traditional practice as "mindless swings" cured by instant per-swing metrics plus gamification — leagues, personal bests, "meaningful at-bats." (2) Batting-fantasy games (MLB Home Run Derby VR) use no pain framing at all — pure fantasy fulfillment of baseball's most thrilling moment, but gated behind a $30 purchase and a headset. (3) Browser-casual commentary articulates zero-friction micro-sessions: no install, no account, click-a-link-and-play in a work break. (4) Single-skill trainers (Monkeytype, Aim Lab) sell honest measurement of one narrow skill — WPM/accuracy-style stats, personal bests, mastery loop, no account.
**Options:**
- **A —** Fantasy-access gap, baseball-first persona. The cage ritual — machine whirr, timed swing, crack, glowing distance readout answering "how far would that one have gone?" — is locked behind physical facilities (drive, booking, per-token cost), $30 VR hardware, or visually disposable 2D/low-poly derby clones; full sims bury pure hitting under rosters and multi-input controls. Deliver the ritual with believable physics and premium visuals, one URL and one key (SPACE) away.
- **B —** Measurable-mastery framing, skill-chaser persona. The Monkeytype/Aim Lab crowd (not necessarily baseball fans) lacks a physics-grounded timing discipline with millisecond feedback ("LATE 23 ms"), per-difficulty windows, personal bests, and an earned 40→90 mph ladder; existing timing games grade with vague stars and streaks.
- **C —** Premium micro-session framing, general desktop persona. Any desk-bound adult wanting a 2–5 minute restorative break; break-time browser games are ad-cluttered and cheap, anything with real game-feel demands installs and commitment. An instantly legible one-button round with disproportionate sensory payoff that runs in a tab — "Slow Roads for baseball."

### Objective function
**Research (precedent: moderate):** HitTrax bills itself as "performance data and entertainment value"; Toptracer states operator metrics (utilization, dwell time, repeat visits); aim trainers (KovaaK's, Aim Lab) optimize measurable skill growth via scored runs, progress graphs, percentile feedback — academically validated as a metrics platform; F2P casual optimizes monetized engagement against hard benchmarks (D1 retention 40%+, 2–10 min sessions, value legible in the first 5–15 minutes); Slow Roads optimizes wow-factor and shareability with 60 fps as the gate. Key mismatch: every comparable monetizes, so their objectives lean revenue/retention; this product is free, local, and telemetry-free, so its objective must be stated via locally verifiable proxies (persisted stats, playtest behavior, performance budgets).
**Options:**
- **A —** Entertainment through visible timing mastery, profit explicitly zero. Maximize "fun per session" from the one-button timing loop, stated as locally verifiable targets: ≤60 s page-load-to-first-pitch; first session naturally sustains ≥3 ten-pitch rounds (~8–12 min); ≥50% "one more token" rate in playtests; median |timing error| at a fixed speed improving ≥30% between first and fifth session; second speed tier unlocked in session one, 90 mph tier within 2–4 hours of cumulative play.
- **B —** Measurable timing-skill development as the product, entertainment as delivery vehicle. Maximize demonstrable reflex improvement: per-speed timing-error distributions visibly tighten session-over-session; unlocks function as graded proficiency exams (e.g., 40→90 mph in 10–15 sessions at ~30 min/day); success is the slope of the improvement curve, not minutes played.
- **C —** Browser-showcase objective. Maximize perceived production value per minute of attention — hard gates: sustained 60 fps at the High preset on target desktop hardware; cold load to first interactive pitch under 10 s; signature audiovisual beats (dramatic lighting, cloth-sim net, LED readout, wood crack/metal ping) all land within the first 30 s. Gameplay depth is satisficed; success is demo impact and shareability.

### Target user(s)
**Research (precedent: moderate):** HitTrax markets to all ages/skills plus entertainment venues; Toptracer explicitly declares a casual/serious dual audience; MLB HRD VR lands with dedicated baseball fans and as a party game with weak solo longevity; Poki's 100M+ monthly players are snack-session casuals (largest cohort 25–34); Slow Roads recruited via tech virality (repeated HN front pages) — developers and web-graphics-curious players; batting-only mobile games target casual baseball fans. Pattern: incumbents go mass-casual or declare a casual/serious dual audience; nobody documents an audience for this exact niche because no incumbent occupies it.
**Options:**
- **A —** Mass-casual instant-play: anyone landing on the URL with a desktop browser and a spacebar; drop-in casuals on 5–15 minute breaks, zero baseball knowledge assumed; must be fully self-explanatory within the first 10-pitch round at 40 mph; the big distance number is the universal hook.
- **B —** Baseball-literate enthusiast primary: the armchair Statcast fan, current/former player, or rec-league hitter who knows what 92 EV at 25° means, playing deliberate solo sessions chasing per-speed personal bests up the 40→90 mph ladder; secondary youth players; names the guaranteed first user explicitly — the project owner on their own machine (build-for-yourself).
- **C —** Showcase-and-shared-machine: the "this runs in a browser?!" web-dev/graphics crowd as the reach channel; co-primary pass-the-keyboard social play — households and office groups competing on the one shared local per-speed high-score board ("beat my 312 ft") as the retention engine.

### Non-goals
**Research (precedent: rich):** Successful comparables are defined by aggressive visible cuts: Homerun Clash and browser derby clones are batting-only (no pitching/fielding/innings); MLB HRD VR ships only the derby despite the full MLB license; Wii Sports Baseball cut baserunning/fielding control — and its most-criticized element was the feature it half-kept as automation (degraded stubs hurt more than clean omission); Slow Roads explicitly frames pared-down design as the thing that enables 60 fps browser visuals (scope cuts as performance strategy). Design-doc convention (Google style): non-goals are plausible rejected goals that fence scope and grant permission to say no — not negated quality requirements.
**Options:**
- **A —** Lean: the three user-deferred features labeled "future ideas, not rejected" (free-practice endless mode, streak challenge, slow-mo replays), plus one umbrella "Batting Cage is not a baseball game" statement; everything else deferred to the spec's later out-of-scope section.
- **B —** Comprehensive categorized fence: PERMANENT non-goals across four categories (game scope: no sim/fielding/pitch variety/stadium play; input: never a second gameplay control; platform: no mobile/touch/VR/native; online/business: no accounts/cloud/multiplayer/ads/IAP/MLB license) cleanly separated from the v1-DEFERRED trio.
- **C —** Principle-anchored design fences: five guardrails each naming the cut and the reason it protects the core — "One button, forever"; "Fastballs only"; "The cage is the entire world"; "Local and private"; "Premium depth over broad reach" — plus the deferred-not-rejected trio and an anti-stub rule (cut features are fully absent, never degraded automation).

## Holistic combinations

### Combination 1 — 1A/2A/3B/4C
"The cage fantasy, honestly measured." Problem: the HitTrax-style cage ritual is real and proven but locked behind physical facilities, VR headsets, or visually disposable clones — deliver it one URL and one SPACE key away. Objective: maximize fun-per-session through visible timing mastery, stated as locally verifiable targets, profit explicitly zero. Users: baseball-literate enthusiasts chasing per-speed personal bests, with the project owner named as the guaranteed first user. Non-goals: five rationale-bearing design fences plus the deferred trio and the anti-stub rule.
**Pros:** Mirrors the product's own conceit — the persona already wants exactly what the locked decisions deliver (indoor facility realism, LED stat board, EV/LA/ms readouts, wood-vs-metal sounds), so every later spec section serves a motivated audience. Objective 2A is the centroid of the guidance ("simple-to-play" lead adjective, token-round arcade structure, zero monetization anywhere) and is measurable without telemetry via the already-mandated local-stats system. 4C's fences carry rationale, giving a no-feedback automated pipeline a decision rule for unanticipated questions ("would this add a second input? then no"); the anti-stub rule encodes the Wii Sports lesson. Naming the project owner as first user makes local-only persistence a deliberate feature and sharpens downstream prioritization. Internally coherent end to end: fantasy persona → fun-through-mastery objective → mastery-chasing user → fences that protect the one-button cage conceit.
**Cons:** Baseball-first persona risks under-weighting novice legibility — later UI sections might assume stat literacy unless an explicit accessibility requirement is added. 2A as written carries no visual/performance gates, so "visually premium" (the guidance's first quality adjective) lacks rubric teeth without supplementation from 2C. Under-emphasizes the shared-machine hot-seat dynamic that the local high-score board structurally supports.
**Objective alignment:** 2A directly serves the guidance's lead requirement and makes the mastery instrumentation the visibility layer of fun rather than the product itself; all later gameplay sections (timing windows, scoring formulas, unlock thresholds) score cleanly against its concrete targets. Gap: visual/audio/performance sections need 2C-style hard gates bolted on as constraints to be scoreable — fixable by co-mingling without changing the maximand.

### Combination 2 — 1B/2B/3B/4B
"The premium timing gym." Problem: skill-chasers lack a physics-grounded timing discipline with honest millisecond measurement. Objective: maximize demonstrable reflex improvement — the improvement-curve slope is success. Users: deliberate solo improvers (baseball-literate or Monkeytype-crowd, lightly adapted to fit). Non-goals: the exhaustive permanent-vs-deferred categorized fence.
**Pros:** The only framing that fully explains why ms-level timing error, the per-speed window table, session history, and performance-gated unlocks are v1 features — they become the core apparatus rather than extras. 2B is the most rigorously measurable objective from local data alone, and unlock thresholds become principled proficiency exams, giving the progression section a clean design rule. 4B is the safest scope artifact for an automated pipeline. Aim-trainer audiences are proven retention-rich and happy with account-free local stats.
**Cons:** Inverts the guidance's evident intent — "game" is the noun and "simple-to-play" the lead adjective; "entertainment as the delivery vehicle" demotes the cage fantasy and the locked premium art direction to packaging. Risks steering later sections toward dashboard-heavy UX and drill-flavored content, in tension with the token-starts-a-round arcade structure the user locked. Improvement-slope success targets (10–15 sessions to 90 mph) are speculative and hostage to playtest variance — a brittle rubric. The "not necessarily baseball fans" persona sits awkwardly with the HitTrax homage, handedness toggle, and wood-vs-metal bat ritual.
**Objective alignment:** Internally tight — problem, objective, and user are all trainer-shaped — but 2B is a mis-weighted rubric for this spec: it scores atmosphere, audio design, and fantasy presentation (half the mandated coverage) as secondary delivery concerns.

### Combination 3 — 1C/2C/3C/4A
"The browser-showcase break." Problem: desk-bound adults lack a premium 2–5 minute break experience in a tab. Objective: maximize perceived production value per minute, with hard perf/presentation gates and gameplay satisficed. Users: the tech-viral "this runs in a browser?!" crowd as reach, pass-the-keyboard hot-seat play as retention (absorbing 3A's occasion-casual into the same audience). Non-goals: lean — the deferred trio plus a "not a baseball game" umbrella.
**Pros:** Gives "visually premium" maximal rubric teeth — the 60 fps gate, sub-10 s load, and signature-beats-in-30 s are the most objectively testable acceptance criteria a no-telemetry product can state. Matches browser distribution, zero-friction token rounds, and the Slow Roads-proven viral channel (presentation as free marketing). Elevates the cloth-sim net, ACES/bloom, and dramatic lighting to first-class requirements. Lean 4A keeps the vision section tight and defers detail to the mandated out-of-scope section.
**Cons:** "Gameplay depth is satisficed" directly conflicts with the locked feature set — per-speed leaderboards, progression unlocks, and a precise timing model demand a gameplay-maximizing rubric, not a satisficing one. The baseball-agnostic occasion framing wastes the HitTrax conceit; this persona has no use for "LATE 23 ms" or per-speed timing windows. Demo-impact/shareability success is not locally verifiable, contradicting the telemetry-free reality. 4A leaves permanent exclusions (multiplayer, mobile, accounts, monetization) unfenced in the Why section — the riskiest choice for a pipeline where no human catches downstream scope creep.
**Objective alignment:** 2C aligns visual/perf sections perfectly but actively mis-scores the gameplay, scoring, and progression sections that constitute most of the spec's mandated coverage.

## Final recommendation
**Rationale:** Combination 1 (1A/2A/3B/4C) is the spine: the guidance is structurally a baseball-fantasy product — the HitTrax conceit, the cage ritual details, the stat readouts — and only 1A/3B explain every locked feature without reframing the product as something it is not (a trainer) or diluting it into something generic (a break toy). Three surgical co-minglings repair Combination 1's identified gaps without breaking coherence. (1) Objective = 2A primary + 2C's hard gates as non-negotiable constraints — this completes the rubric: fun-through-visible-mastery scores every gameplay/scoring/progression decision, the presentation gates score every visual/audio/performance decision, and neither is tradeable against the other. 2B's improvement instrumentation already lives inside 2A's mastery targets. (2) Target users = 3B primary + explicit secondary tiers absorbing 3A's hard legibility requirement (self-explanatory first round) and 3C's pass-the-keyboard hot-seat dynamic, with a stated tie-break rule so downstream sections never face an ambiguous persona conflict. (3) Non-goals = 4C's rationale-bearing fences with 4B's explicit permanent exclusions enumerated inside fences 4 and 5, so the automated pipeline is fully fenced while the Why section stays principle-shaped; the user's three deferred features remain "future ideas, not rejected" verbatim. The problem statement is 1A with one clause absorbing 1C's zero-friction occasion (a token round fits a two-minute break). Upstream consistency: all locked Q&A decisions (camera, indoor setting, two modes, stats extras, handedness/bat choice, the three deferred features) and the tech direction (browser, keyboard-first, 60 fps, quality presets) are restated or reinforced, never contradicted; the objective's metrics need only local stats and playtests, consistent with local-only, telemetry-free persistence.

**Final Why components:**
- **Problem statement:** The batting cage has a specific, proven pleasure: the whirr of the machine, one perfectly timed swing, the crack of contact, and a glowing board answering the question every hitter asks — "how far would that one have gone?" HitTrax facilities and Toptracer ranges built businesses on exactly this loop of instant per-swing feedback turned into a game. But for someone at a desktop, that experience is locked away. Real cages demand a drive, a booking, and per-token fees; MLB Home Run Derby VR demands a $30 purchase and a headset; browser home-run-derby clones are visually disposable 2D/low-poly toys with arcade physics; and full baseball sims bury pure hitting under rosters, pitching meters, and multi-input control schemes. No product delivers the cage ritual itself — believable ball flight, a net that reacts, an honest stat board — with premium presentation, one URL and one key away. Batting Cage closes that gap: press SPACE to time your swing against a pitching machine, and a 10-pitch token round delivers the whole ritual in about two minutes — short enough for a break, deep enough to chase for months.
- **Objective function:** Primary maximand — fun per session from the one-button timing loop, made visible as mastery. The game is free, local, and telemetry-free; profit is explicitly zero; success is measured by locally verifiable proxies (the mandated persistent-stats system plus hands-on playtests). Targets: (a) time-to-fun — cold load to interactive in ≤10 s, page load to first pitch thrown in ≤60 s; (b) session pull — a first session naturally sustains ≥3 ten-pitch rounds (~8–12 min, in line with casual first-session benchmarks), and ≥50% of completed playtest rounds are followed by starting another token; (c) visible mastery — median |timing error| at a fixed machine speed improves ≥30% between a player's first and fifth session, the median player unlocks a second speed tier in session one and reaches the 90 mph tier within 2–4 hours of cumulative play. Hard presentation gates — non-negotiable constraints, never traded against the fun targets: sustained 60 fps at the High quality preset on target desktop hardware, and the signature audiovisual beats (dramatic cage lighting, cloth-sim net reaction, LED-board readout, wood crack / metal ping) all landing within the first 30 seconds of play. Rubric for the rest of this spec: every gameplay, scoring, and progression decision is scored against fun-through-visible-mastery; every visual, audio, and performance decision is scored against the presentation gates.
- **Target user(s):** Primary — the baseball-literate enthusiast: the armchair Statcast fan, current or former player, or rec-league hitter who knows what 92 EV at 25° means, has stood in a real cage (or wishes one were nearby), and plays deliberate solo sessions chasing per-speed personal bests up the 40→90 mph ladder. The guaranteed first user is named explicitly: the project owner on their own machine — a build-for-yourself product, which is why local-only persistence is a feature, not a gap. Secondary — (a) the drop-in casual with zero baseball knowledge, whose presence imposes a hard requirement: the game must be fully self-explanatory within the first 10-pitch round at 40 mph, with the LED board's big distance number as the universal hook; (b) pass-the-keyboard shared-machine play — household or office hot-seat competition on the local per-speed high-score board ("beat my 312 ft"), enabled by one-key input and fast round restart. Tie-break rule: when personas conflict, design for the enthusiast's mastery loop; the secondary personas contribute legibility and hot-seat constraints, never feature additions.
- **Non-goals:** Five permanent design fences, each carrying its rationale so downstream sections can resolve unanticipated questions without reopening scope. (1) One button, forever — no second gameplay input: no aiming, power meters, swing types, or mouse/motion swings; depth comes from timing windows and machine speed, never from more controls. (2) Fastballs only — no breaking balls, location variance, or pitch selection; fastball-over-the-plate is the design, not a gap; difficulty scales solely through machine speed and tighter timing windows. (3) The cage is the entire world — no stadiums, open fields, or fly-out cameras; every batted ball ends in the netting or on the cage floor, and the LED board is the only window to the open field (the HitTrax conceit is preserved, never bypassed). (4) Local and private — no servers, accounts, cloud sync, online leaderboards, multiplayer, telemetry, ads, in-app purchases, or licensed MLB content; all data stays on the player's machine. (5) Premium depth over broad reach — desktop browser, keyboard-first: no mobile/touch, no VR, no native/Steam build in v1; 60 fps desktop fidelity is chosen over portability. Deferred, not rejected (future ideas per the user's decision): free-practice endless mode, streak challenge, slow-motion replays. Anti-stub rule: cut features are fully absent — never shipped as degraded automation or placeholders. The spec's later out-of-scope/future section expands these fences into an exhaustive list.
