# What — Working Document

## Per-element research and options

### Core functionality
**Research (precedent: rich):** Comparables converge on four structures. (1) HitTrax — the named fantasy reference — gives every swing an instant stat card (EV, LA, distance), runs built-in game modes (5-swing derby rounds, quality-hit contests, leagues), and auto-records stats/leaderboards with percentiles; its "entertainment mode" notably discards session data. (2) Toptracer Range ships named modes (Longest Drive, Points Game) scored from distance/accuracy with per-mode leaderboards and app-held shot history — the gamified-practice template. (3) MLB The Show is the timing-feedback standard: every swing gets a banded verbal verdict (Very Early → Very Late), establishing graded, directional per-pitch feedback as the expected vocabulary. (4) Homerun Clash proves a one-input swing graded Perfect/Good/OK sustains a whole product. (5) MLB HRD VR retains via a 100-level XP ladder plus achievement quests — meta-economy, not skill-gating. (6) Wii Sports Baseball's "Hitting Home Runs" training is the cleanest structural precedent for the locked design: exactly 10 fastballs per round, round score, medal thresholds (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum, Platinum adding an aggregate-distance bar). Synthesis: (a) per-swing graded feedback with named bands, (b) fixed-length rounds scored by distance × quality, (c) per-difficulty leaderboards, (d) progression via score/medal gates or XP economies.
**Options:**
- **A —** Arcade-cabinet purist — the cage IS the interface. One-key play (SPACE, timestamped at keydown); six diegetic speed tiers (40–90 mph) with telegraphed cadence (whirr + feed clunk); five outcome bands (WHIFF/FOUL TIP/WEAK/SOLID/FLUSH) from signed timing error with early=pull/late=oppo; full physical flight + net reaction + balls persist on floor; LED board per swing (distance headline, EV, LA, "LATE 23 ms"); token round of 10 pitches, score = Σ(quality multiplier × projected distance); per-speed top-10 with three-initial arcade entry; raw-score thresholds unlock the next tier (greyed-out tiers show their unlock score); local persistence (per-speed highs, PBs, lifetime averages); diegetic bat rack (righty/lefty, wood/metal); attract mode. No menus beyond a minimal pause/quality overlay.
- **B —** Graded round loop — Wii-Sports-medal structure on the locked spine. Everything in A's items 1–4 and 8, plus: named per-swing grades in The Show's vocabulary (PERFECT/GREAT/GOOD/JUST EARLY–JUST LATE/FOUL/MISS) so casuals read words while enthusiasts read milliseconds; explicit round lifecycle (token → spin-up → 10 pitches → end-of-round recap with a 10-cell pitch-by-pitch strip, round score, medal earned); Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum medals per speed at score thresholds (Platinum adds an aggregate-distance bar); medals — not raw score — are the progression currency (Bronze at tier N unlocks N+1: "get Bronze at 50"); New-PB and first-medal callouts with light/sound beats; per-speed top-10 with initials + one-key rematch; persistence includes medals, per-speed PBs/averages, and a session history log (date, rounds, best score, median timing error); first-run nudge is a single diegetic "INSERT TOKEN — PRESS SPACE" prompt at 40 mph.
- **C —** Stat-locker mastery loop — HitTrax fantasy turned all the way up. A's items 1–5 and 8, plus: full per-swing stat line logged (EV, LA, carry, signed ε ms, spray PULL/CENTER/OPPO); a persistent local "locker" (per-speed PBs, rolling averages, last-50-swings timing-error sparkline on the board, full session log); consistency-gated progression (tier N+1 unlocks via ≥7 balls in play AND round-median |ε| under a per-speed threshold, with live progress shown); per-speed distance "clubs" (300 FT CLUB) as named board celebrations; post-round board cycle with trend delta vs last five rounds ("−8 ms vs avg"); every swing ever taken stored locally — unlike HitTrax's throwaway entertainment mode, nothing is discarded. Flagged tension: analytics drift toward the trainer framing the Why declined; median-based unlocks are less legible to the casual.

### UI / UX
**Research (precedent: rich):** Six precedent clusters. (1) HitTrax bay screens — instant per-swing stat card, session leaderboards, spray views on one big monitor; the screen IS the interface, glanced at between swings. (2) Toptracer bays: walk in, pick mode on the bay screen, hit; guest-first, no account. (3) Dead Space is the canonical fully diegetic UI ("diegetic by design and by implementation"); Callisto Protocol analyses stress in-world UI works only when surfaces are purpose-built for readability. (4) MLB HRD VR (closest product) drew documented criticism for missing selection feedback on in-world menus — a concrete pitfall list for diegetic panels. (5) MLB The Show delivers timing feedback as an immediate post-swing toast. (6) Slow Roads defines the premium-browser shell: one click to playing, no sign-in, minimal auto-hiding overlay; classic arcade attract-mode + 3-initial entry is the proven idiom for shared-machine score chasing.
**Options:**
- **A —** "The cage is the menu" — one continuous 3D scene, zero 2D chrome. Page loads into an attract state (camera drift, machine idling, board cycling PRESS SPACE + top scores); every interaction is an in-world station reached by automatic camera moves: machine control panel (lit tier buttons with lock lights and stenciled unlock thresholds; arrows move a glowing focus highlight, SPACE confirms with a click), token slot, bat rack (handedness by physically switching batter's boxes), and the LED board multiplexing dot-matrix pages (last-swing card, round score, high scores, PBs, history). Round summary is a camera push-in on the board. FTUE is board-driven dot-matrix prompts. Only flat elements: auto-fading key-hint footer and a first-run quality auto-detect toast. Settings on a wall-mounted breaker-style panel. Mitigation for HRD VR pitfall: focus highlights + audio confirms; "SPACE again = same speed" instant rematch.
- **B —** "Diegetic stage, minimal shell" — in-play UI 100% diegetic (LED board only), everything outside the live round is a thin 2D overlay styled as facility signage, in four states: Attract (logo, PRESS SPACE), Ready (bottom control strip: tiers via arrows, B = bat, H = handedness, SPACE = token), Round (overlay hidden), Summary (compact card: score, grade, NEW BEST, SPACE = again). ESC settings sheet; TAB stats sheet with HitTrax-style cards and session-history table. Mouse optional everywhere; persistent key hints; FTUE = three contextual captions; tab-blur auto-pauses. Fastest path: load → SPACE → SPACE = first pitch inside 20 s. Trade: between rounds the fantasy yields to flat sheets.
- **C —** "Arcade hall" — coin-op ritual in a realistic facility. True attract mode (orbit, board cycling per-speed TOP 5 with initials, INSERT TOKEN); token = credit; speed select staged as game select on the machine panel; summary is a full board-takeover (score count-up, grade stamp); initials entry on the board only when cracking a top 5 (arrows + SPACE, skippable, defaults to last-used initials so solo flow stays one-key). PBs/averages/history on a second smaller cage-side monitor (TAB); bat/handedness at the rack; one minimal ESC overlay for system settings. Dot-matrix typography, no neon camp. Risks: initials friction for the solo enthusiast; arcade register must avoid clashing with the realistic-facility tone.

### Inputs & outputs
**Research (precedent: rich):** Inputs: Doodle Baseball — the canonical zero-friction comparable — uses literally one desktop input (spacebar) and starts in seconds; rhythm-game practice (Playdate dev forum, exceed7) is to timestamp input at event time and judge against a stable clock, since frame-quantized input at 60 fps adds up to ~16.7 ms error — as large as a typical PERFECT window. Outputs: HitTrax emits a structured per-swing record (EV, LA, distance, outcome) plus portable session reports (print/email) — portable stats are part of the product; The Show shows a per-swing timing class with EV/LA. Persistence: web-game best practice (bugnet.io; itch.io devlogs) is localStorage with a schema version, writes on visibilitychange/beforeunload, plus user-initiated export/import because browser storage is evictable ("borrowed, not owned"); casual derby clones skip this and lose saves.
**Options:**
- **A —** Strict diegetic keyboard-only I/O. SPACE only for gameplay (also token/advance), event-time stamped, key-repeat ignored, one swing per pitch; arrows + Enter run the in-world panel (speed, handedness, bat, quality, volume); Esc pauses; blur auto-pauses and voids/re-feeds in-flight pitch. No mouse, no remap, no gamepad, no touch. Outputs: rendered scene with locked depth cues, locked audio set, per-pitch LED card (carry ft, EV, LA, signed ms, outcome label), round score + per-speed board, one versioned localStorage record. Nothing leaves the page: no files, no clipboard, no network. Weakness: ignores storage evictability — the enthusiast's months-long PB career at risk.
- **B —** Keyboard-first with mouse parity and durable, portable data. Gameplay identical to A (SPACE only, event-time, lockout); menus/panel additionally mouse-clickable (browser convention, faster hot-seat handoff); Esc pause, M mute; blur auto-pause + pitch void. Each pitch emits a structured swing record (pitch #, speed, signed ε ms, outcome class, EV, LA, distance, points) that drives the board and appends to history; persistence is a versioned JSON document in localStorage written at round end and on visibilitychange; Settings offers Export Save (.json download) and Import Save (file picker). Zero network: the save file is a user-action artifact, so "local and private" holds. Mouse parity is not a second gameplay input — the fence bans gameplay inputs (aiming, meters, mouse swings).
- **C —** Formal I/O contract plus hot-seat share outputs. Fully enumerated event contract: SPACE keydown is the sole gameplay event (performance.now-stamped, debounced, accepted release → plate + grace; no input = recorded TAKE); every other binding listed and reserved (arrows/Enter, Esc, M, H, B, 1–6 direct tier select, F3 diagnostics); mouse in menus. Outputs: B's record + save, plus (1) end-of-round shareable text summary copied to clipboard on demand ("BATTING CAGE — 70 MPH — 8/10 — BEST 312 FT — SCORE 4,120"), (2) optional diagnostics overlay (fps, frame time, input-to-judgment latency) making the 60 fps gate observable, (3) per-session aggregates (median |ε|, hard-hit %, contact %) persisted to feed the objective's mastery metrics. Risk: shortcut sprawl (1–6, H, B) competes with the diegetic panel fiction.

### Primary user flow(s)
**Research (precedent: rich):** The market splits into select-first flows (HitTrax kiosk → mode → token buys fixed ball count, D-BAT 15 pitches/token, feeds every ~5–10 s; Toptracer bay touchscreen; HRD VR menus → 90 s round → ceremony → XP meta) and play-first flows (Doodle Baseball: zero menu, loads straight to the plate, instant restart; classic arcade: attract demo + INSERT COIN + cycling high scores → coin → play → initials → attract). Universal elements regardless of camp: per-swing instant stat card, end-of-round totals, leaderboard/initials moment, meta layer between rounds. Slow Roads shows premium browser 3D needs exactly one splash click (audio unlock + asset-init buffer; 97% of users < 10 s). The 10-pitch token at a chosen speed maps almost 1:1 onto the real-cage token flow and the arcade attract loop.
**Options:**
- **A —** ONE master flow — "The Cage Visit," a single fully diegetic arcade-cabinet loop; first run, returning, and hot-seat are inline variations. Cold load → one-click splash ("STEP INTO THE CAGE") → cage ≤10 s, board attract; setup at the panel (arrows, green/red tier LEDs with printed unlock requirements, H/B); ENTER drops token, wheels spin up, "PITCH 1/10"; per-pitch beat ×10 (clunk → release → flight cues → SPACE → contact/whiff → board flash + running score); round end (totals, unlock klaxon, top-5 initials); board returns to attract; re-token or close tab (per-pitch autosave). First run handled by board prompts synced to the first pitches — no tutorial screens, no menus.
- **B —** THREE persona-mapped end-to-end scenarios, each ending in measurable success criteria (ready-made acceptance tests). Flow 1 "First Contact" (drop-in casual, cold machine): defaults 40 mph/righty/wood, one offered action, first pitch ≤60 s, diegetic coaching, full payoff inside 30 s, unlock tease; success = completes round one unaided and starts round two. Flow 2 "Mastery Session" (enthusiast, returning): auto-resume loadout, 70 mph metal, full stat lines, 3–5 rounds, 90 mph unlock ceremony, session summary persisted; success = ≥3 rounds, mastery metrics recorded. Flow 3 "Pass-the-Keyboard Duel": re-token with 1 key ≤10 s, beat the mark, initials on the top-5, attract cycles the rivalry; success = handoff ≤2 keys, no menus.
- **C —** TWO flows split by altitude. Flow 1 "The Pitch Cycle" (the ~7–9 s atom, ×10/token) as a strict state sequence: ARMED → FEED (clunk) → RELEASE (cue flash at fixed point) → FLIGHT (≈0.55–1.3 s by tier; growth + shadow cues) → INPUT (timestamped SPACE in the per-speed window; none = take) → OUTCOME branch (signed-ε model) → RESOLUTION (flight, cloth-sim net, ball settles) → BOARD REVEAL (distance headline then EV/LA/timing, ~1.5 s hold — the climax beat) → next FEED. Flow 2 "The Visit" (session macro): splash → cage → SETUP → repeat [token → Pitch Cycle ×10 → totals + ceremonies] → between-rounds board pages (stats, history, unlock progress) → depart implicitly (per-pitch autosave; resume on return). First-run and hot-seat are parameterizations of Flow 2, not separate flows.

## Holistic combinations

### Combination 1 — 1A/2A/3A/4A
"The Pure Cabinet." The most literal execution of diegetic-first: one continuous 3D scene with zero 2D chrome, keyboard-only I/O, raw-score tier unlocks stenciled on the machine panel, all data living on the LED board, and a single master Cage Visit flow covering every situation inline. Nothing leaves the page — no mouse, no files, no clipboard.
**Pros:** Maximum spend of the premium-presentation differentiator — the attract state itself satisfies the 30-second signature-beats gate; perfectly coherent fiction (in a real facility the control box and HitTrax board ARE the UI); smallest interaction surface to spec and build; one-key culture absolutely intact; single flow keeps the spec tight; fastest possible path to first pitch.
**Cons:** 3A knowingly ignores browser-storage evictability — one cache clear erases the 90 mph unlock and every PB, directly attacking the visible-mastery maximand for the build-for-yourself primary user; raw-score unlock thresholds ("post 12,400 at 50") are less legible than a one-word condition; five outcome bands without The Show's graded vocabulary make casuals read numbers, straining the locked self-explanatory-first-round requirement; the LED board alone cannot comfortably host session history and trend data the enthusiast persona chases; no mouse parity adds needless friction on panels; a single flow gives downstream physics/audio/UI sections no per-pitch state machine to hang exact timings on.
**Objective alignment:** Strongest on the hard presentation gates and time-to-fun (≤10 s / ≤60 s trivially met). Weakest on visible mastery — the maximand: data-loss risk, no grade vocabulary, and thin trend feedback under-serve "median |ε| improves ≥30% by session five" and the months-long PB chase. Session pull is adequate via attract + instant rematch.

### Combination 2 — 1B/2B/3B/4B
"The Legible Loop." The objective function transcribed into features: named per-swing grades (PERFECT → MISS) + Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum medals as the progression currency, a thin facility-signage 2D shell outside the live round (in-round stays 100% diegetic), durable versioned saves with export/import and mouse parity, and three persona-mapped flows that double as acceptance tests.
**Pros:** Best direct coverage of every objective target — grade words make mastery visible to casuals while milliseconds serve enthusiasts; "get Bronze at 50" is the most legible unlock condition of the three; export/import protects the long-horizon PB career the maximand depends on; the TAB stats sheet handles enthusiast data density trivially; persona flows hand the no-feedback pipeline ready-made success criteria; cheapest, lowest-risk build; load → SPACE → SPACE = first pitch in ~20 s.
**Cons:** Spends the least of the premium-diegetic differentiator — between rounds the fantasy yields to flat sheets exactly where the marketplace gap (premium presentation, "this runs in a browser?") lives, and the locked guidance explicitly mandates diegetic-first UI with the machine panel for speed select; facility-signage overlays risk reading as generic web-game chrome; three persona flows overlap heavily (the same loop narrated three times) and still never define the per-pitch state machine downstream sections need most.
**Objective alignment:** Strongest on the fun-through-visible-mastery rubric (grades, medals, durable history, legible unlocks) and on time-to-fun/session-pull targets. Meets but does not maximize the presentation gates: 60 fps and 30-s beats still land in-round, but the between-round experience trades the differentiator for convention — a real cost given the gates are constraints and presentation is the market positioning.

### Combination 3 — 1C/2C/3C/4C
"The Measured Arcade." HitTrax analytics turned all the way up inside an arcade-hall frame: every swing ever taken logged with spray/trend data, consistency-gated progression (balls in play + round-median |ε| thresholds), a second cage-side stats monitor, board-takeover ceremonies with initials entry, a formally enumerated I/O contract with clipboard share + diagnostics overlay, and two flows split by altitude (Pitch Cycle atom + Visit macro).
**Pros:** Deepest service to the tie-break-winning enthusiast persona — the locker, sparkline trends, and per-speed clubs put the objective's own mastery numbers on screen; 4C's pitch-cycle state machine is the best downstream skeleton in any option (physics, audio, and UI sections hang exact timings on it); 3C's contract converts I/O into testable acceptance criteria and the F3 overlay makes the 60 fps gate observable instead of a vibe; the second monitor solves LED data density diegetically; clipboard summary serves "beat my 312 ft" with zero network.
**Cons:** Consistency-gated unlocks (median-|ε| thresholds) are illegible inside a first 40 mph round — a direct violation of the locked casual hard requirement — and drift toward the measurable-skill-trainer framing the Why explicitly declined; a player who barrels but scatters can stall on the gate, threatening the 2–4-hour ramp to 90 mph; 1–6 direct tier-select sprawl competes with the diegetic machine-panel fiction the user locked; arcade-hall register needs careful art direction to avoid clashing with the realistic-training-facility tone; initials ceremony adds friction the solo enthusiast never asked for (mitigated by last-used default, but still ceremony-first).
**Objective alignment:** Best mastery instrumentation on paper, but the unlock mechanism mis-scores against the rubric twice — it breaks first-round legibility (locked requirement) and reframes progression as proficiency examination rather than fun-made-visible. Presentation gates well served by the attract mode; time-to-fun fine.

## Final recommendation
**Rationale:** Spine = 1B/2A/3B/4C, with four surgical grafts; no single column survives contact with the locked Why intact. Core functionality must be 1B: named grades + medal gates are the only progression mechanism that makes mastery visible to the enthusiast AND legible to the casual in one word ("get Bronze at 50"), satisfying both the maximand and the locked self-explanatory-first-round requirement — but it absorbs 1A's arcade ritual (attract mode, token ceremony, initials entry, diegetic bat rack) and 1C's stat locker stripped of its consistency gate (full swing log, spray tags, trend sparkline, distance clubs): the analytics become celebration and visibility, never a gate, which is exactly where the Why drew the trainer line. UI/UX must be 2A, not 2B: the guidance explicitly mandates diegetic-first with the machine panel and LED board as the interface, the marketplace positioning is premium presentation, and the hard gates are non-negotiable constraints — 2B's flat between-round sheets spend the differentiator to buy convenience that 2A's instant-rematch shortcut already provides; 2A absorbs 2C's second cage-side stats monitor (solving the one real readability limit of a dot-matrix board, per the Callisto lesson that diegetic surfaces must be purpose-built for their content) and 2C's initials-default-to-last-used, and concedes exactly one flat surface — the ESC system sheet — under a stated rule: if it exists in a real training facility it lives in the scene; if it exists only because this is a browser app (quality preset, volumes, save export/import, reset) it lives on the ESC sheet. I/O must be 3B: event-time timestamped SPACE plus a versioned, exportable save is the difference between a months-long PB career and a cache-clear tragedy (the build-for-yourself first user is precisely who loses); it absorbs 3C's per-session aggregates (the objective's own metrics, persisted), TAKE recording, F3 diagnostics (makes the 60 fps gate observable), and the clipboard round summary (serves the hot-seat persona with zero network) — while rejecting 3C's 1–6 shortcut sprawl that fights the diegetic panel. Flows must be 4C: in a one-button game the per-pitch beat IS the product, and the Pitch Cycle state machine is the skeleton every downstream section (physics timings, audio beats, board reveals) hangs on; the Visit macro absorbs 4A's attract-loop detail, and 4B's three persona scenarios are kept as named parameterizations with success criteria — the acceptance-test value without narrating the same loop three times. Upstream consistency: every locked decision is restated, the five fences hold (SPACE remains the sole gameplay input; mouse/arrows/TAB are UI affordances the fence's own text permits; export and clipboard are user-initiated local artifacts, no network; balls never leave the cage; the deferred trio stays fully absent per the anti-stub rule), and the objective's targets appear verbatim as flow success criteria.
